The problem

Metro flow forecasting needs model representations of dynamic transport relationships.

Inputs, process and outputs

Inputs
Metro network structure, flows and temporal variation.
Process
The joint study learns representations through masked-flow, station-relation and distance tasks, then hierarchically combines time, station and route signals.
Outputs
Transport forecasting methods and a Knowledge-Based Systems article.

Research questions and team methods

Turn metro structure into learnable tasks

Transfer and regular stations differ; fixed network representations cannot fully reflect time-varying flow.

The joint study used multiple pretraining tasks for station and spatial relationships, combining masked flow, station sequences and distance before transferring representations to a forecasting model.

The team completed dynamic transport methods and comparative experiments, producing a Knowledge-Based Systems article.

Combine time, nearby stations and routes hierarchically

Flattening nearby stations can lose shared-route and transfer-direction structure.

The joint study used MLAN to hierarchically integrate time, station and route signals, combining pretrained representations with GRU features and comparative validation.

The team developed comparable transport-network forecasting methods. My scope was code and technical support.

Results and validation

Produced a formal Knowledge-Based Systems journal article in 2024.
