Context and objectives

Convert DNA signals at different scales into testable 3D chromatin interaction predictions.

  • Research question: can a multi-scale design use local sequence signals of different lengths together for prediction?
  • Validation path: keep data settings consistent, compare models and ablations, then examine cross-dataset behavior and explainability.

Key design decisions

Capture DNA signals at different scales

ChallengePredicting chromatin interactions from sequence alone requires local motifs and longer-range signals.

Approach and tradeoffsI designed Inception-based multi-scale feature extraction with ablation, implementing parallel convolutions and aggregation while preserving kernel, channel and pooling configurations. Building on earlier research, I organized H5 preprocessing caches, separate logs and model files, and parameter exploration for more systematic thesis comparisons.

ResultCompleted the multi-scale model, comparison workflow and thesis, allowing architectural choices to be evaluated separately.

Do not assume large pretrained models suit every dataset

ChallengeDNA-structure prediction across cell types may need different representations; pretraining scale does not establish suitability.

Approach and tradeoffsI compared cell datasets and a DNABERT2 baseline on GM12878 and HeLaS3, preserving data, model settings and baseline workflows to examine applicability.

ResultThe thesis records weaker DNABERT2-512 performance on HeLaS3 under these settings. This does not generalize to all pretrained models.

Look beyond scores to sequence sensitivity

ChallengePrediction scores do not explain whether shifting an anchor destabilizes the method.

Approach and tradeoffsI used DeepLIFT to inspect sequence contributions and limitations of long sequences and anchor positions, comparing salient regions and documenting center-shift observations.

ResultThe thesis identifies stability limitations of anchor-based methods under center shifts and retains the interpretation boundary.

More technical detail

How multi-scale convolutions form features

Inputs are a pair of DNA sequences and an interaction label. Parallel convolution branches extract local signals at different lengths. Two Inception feature-extraction layers and configurable aggregation form predictive features; ablation examines the effect of each design.

Reproducible ablation and its limits

Configurations compare a length-9 branch with parallel length-3/5/7/9 branches. Both retain 1×1 and pooling branches, with 100 total channels and the same learning rate, training limit and data settings. Parameter counts still require separate control; configuration alone does not prove a scale effect or superiority.

Data splits and third-party methods

Comparison workflows share train/dev/test splits and retain entry points for BaseCNN, MCIENet, DeepLIFT and format conversion. Shared splits do not establish non-overlapping genomic regions or cross-cell generalization. Some preprocessing builds on ChINN, and comparison entry points do not imply all baselines were completed.
