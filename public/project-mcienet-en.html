Core problems and design decisions

Capture DNA signals at different scales

Predicting chromatin interactions from sequence alone requires local motifs and longer-range signals.

I designed Inception-based multi-scale feature extraction with ablation, implementing parallel convolutions and aggregation while preserving kernel, channel and pooling configurations. Building on earlier research, I organized H5 preprocessing caches, separate logs and model files, and parameter exploration for more systematic thesis comparisons.

Completed the multi-scale model, comparison workflow and thesis, allowing architectural choices to be evaluated separately.

Do not assume large pretrained models suit every dataset

DNA-structure prediction across cell types may need different representations; pretraining scale does not establish suitability.

I compared cell datasets and a DNABERT2 baseline on GM12878 and HeLaS3, preserving data, model settings and baseline workflows to examine applicability.

The thesis records weaker DNABERT2-512 performance on HeLaS3 under these settings. This does not generalize to all pretrained models.

Look beyond scores to sequence sensitivity

Prediction scores do not explain whether shifting an anchor destabilizes the method.

I used DeepLIFT to inspect sequence contributions and limitations of long sequences and anchor positions, comparing salient regions and documenting center-shift observations.

The thesis identifies stability limitations of anchor-based methods under center shifts and retains the interpretation boundary.

Technical details and validation scope

My responsibilities

Research question and architecture, data pipeline integration, experiments and evaluation, thesis writing and repository engineering.

What multi-scale design tests

Inputs are paired genomic DNA sequences and interaction labels. Parallel convolution branches examine local sequence patterns of different lengths and aggregate them into predictive features. The code includes two Inception extraction layers and configurable aggregation. Ablation makes the value of each design testable rather than merely changing a model name.

A reproducible ablation comparison

Configurations compare a length-9 extraction kernel with parallel 3/5/7/9 kernels. Both retain 1×1 information-preserving and pooling branches, 100 total channels, and the same learning rate, training limit and input settings. This defines a reproducible comparison; parameter counts still require separate control, so configuration alone does not prove a scale effect or superiority.

Validation and sample unit

The classification sample is a sequence pair. Ablation, cross-dataset evaluation and interpretability examine model behavior. Later comparison scripts preserve train/dev/test splits from the same data. Shared splits avoid changing samples between methods but do not prove nonoverlapping genomic regions or universal cross-cell generalization.

Make comparison experiments reproducible

The pipeline uses GM12878 and HeLa-S3 CTCF data as examples. Separate entries train BaseCNN and MCIENet and run DeepLIFT. Later scripts convert the same data.h5 train/dev/test splits into ChINN and DNABERT2 formats and normalize test metrics to a common format.

The code and configurations support consistent inputs, reruns and result checks. Cross-cell generalization remains limited, and retained comparison entry points do not imply that every baseline was completed or outperformed.

Method attribution

Some positive/negative sample preprocessing was adapted and integrated from ChINN. Third-party foundations are distinguished from my model design.
