Context and objectives

Survey dispersion affects travel and budgets, requiring computable distances and representative routes.

System architecture and workflow

Architecture overview

Inputs
Sample addresses and interviewer assignments; lists and real coordinates are not displayed publicly.
Process
Group by survey area, build pairwise matrices, retain missing-value rules and solve TSP separately per area.
Outputs
Total and average distance, visit sequence and route maps for survey planning.

Workflow diagram

Key design decisions

Constrain routes to each interviewer's real assignment

ChallengeSample addresses are dispersed. One shortest route across all areas may not reflect real work assignments.

Approach and tradeoffsDefined areas by district and interviewer ID, merged coordinates, built per-area distance matrices and used a route solver to produce visit sequences.

ResultProduced total and average distances, sample sequences and maps per survey area for planning.

Keep planning possible when route distances are missing

ChallengeWalking distances can be missing for many address pairs. Straight-line substitutes alone underestimate travel.

Approach and tradeoffsRetained straight-line baselines and estimated missing routes at 1.5 times that distance, storing retrieval results and completing both directions and self-distances for a usable matrix.

ResultKept planning from stopping at missing values. The coefficient was a historical rule, not a validated road-distance calibration.

Validation and limits

Missing routes use the historical estimation rule. Outputs support route and budget planning; actual travel requires calibration.
