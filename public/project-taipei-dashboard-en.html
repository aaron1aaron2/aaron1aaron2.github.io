Context and objectives

Disaster facilities, population and tourism data were scattered across sources. Facility totals alone do not reveal concentration or gaps around attractions. The team used population needs and spatial distribution to make these differences comparable.

System architecture and workflow

Architecture overview

Inputs
Public data on district population, attractions, shelter capacity and disaster facilities.
Process
The team compared district allocation and resources near attractions separately, answering questions at different spatial scales.
Outputs
A city-analysis dashboard and finalist project for comparing and discussing resource differences.

Key design decisions

Ask who resources serve, beyond facility counts

ChallengeFacility counts alone cannot compare population needs with resources around tourism hotspots.

Approach and tradeoffsI handled the concept, specifications and presentation, turning population, attractions and disaster resources into discussable requirements and aligning the proposal direction.

ResultDefined team analysis directions for district allocation, shelter capacity and facilities near attractions, completing the proposal and reaching the final.

Separate district allocation from attraction-level needs

ChallengePopulation, shelter capacity and attraction locations use different spatial scales. One totals table cannot answer both district allocation and nearby-resource questions.

Approach and tradeoffsI organized proposal needs and perspectives into specifications and delivered the presentation. The team handled data and visualization for district population/resources, shelter capacity and facilities near attractions.

ResultReaders can inspect district allocation and attraction surroundings separately, with an appropriate view for each question.
