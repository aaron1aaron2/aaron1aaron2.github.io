The problem

Fault pattern, severity and degradation time answer different questions; incomplete data requires explicit uncertainty.

Inputs, process and outputs

Inputs
Equipment context, sampling and observations, and operating and waveform coverage.
Process
Separate operating detection, anomaly patterns, severity and extrapolation, checking the denominator in anomaly ratios.
Outputs

Scope and limitations

Fixed time-bin sampling remains a research comparison. Interpret conclusions within data coverage and operating conditions.

Key problems and my approach

Pattern, severity and lifetime answer different questions

A single health score combining fault class, vibration level and lifetime makes it unclear whether a value represents cause, degree or time.

I led a layered sequence from equipment information and preprocessing through operating detection, classification, ISO assessment, RUL and reporting, retaining each layer's data and confidence.

Established an interpretable, traceable workflow that limits or stops inference when evidence is insufficient and states the supported level of conclusion.

Ask what the anomaly percentage divides by

Taking only an extreme value per operating segment is easy, but gives short and long segments equal weight instead of representing exposure time fairly.

I responded to stakeholder challenges with time-bin comparisons while acknowledging incomplete raw-waveform coverage. I separated event, time and observed-waveform ratios and compared bias, coverage and system costs.

Brought statistical definitions and production-data limits into decisions. Time-bin sampling remains research; final algorithm selection is not claimed.

Results and validation

Established layered diagnostics, insufficient-data fallback rules and sampling comparisons. Sampling strategy remains under study.
